# Q1 — `/ToUnicode` coverage

Spec §18, question Q1:

> Across the corpus, what fraction of embedded fonts have a usable `/ToUnicode`
> CMap? Subset LaTeX fonts frequently do not. If coverage is below roughly 85%,
> step 6 of §7.2 (glyph-name heuristics) and possibly a shape-matching fallback
> become Phase 3 work rather than Phase 8.
> *Cost: a weekend. Value: potentially a quarter.* Do this first.

**Answer: 53.0%, decisively below the threshold — but the spec's diagnosis of
*why* is wrong, and the remedy it proposes is aimed at the wrong step.**

Reproduce with:

```bash
./corpus/fetch.sh                  # pdf.js, Apache-2.0
pwsh corpus/latex/build.ps1        # 13 LaTeX samples, needs MiKTeX or TeX Live
cargo run --release -p rasura-fontsurvey
```

---

## The headline number

1390 embedded fonts across 992 documents (1201 of them subsets):

| `/ToUnicode` state | fonts | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| usable | 737 | 53.0% |
| absent | 593 | 42.7% |
| empty | 45 | 3.2% |
| degenerate | 8 | 0.6% |
| undecodable | 7 | 0.5% |

"Usable" means it resolves, decodes, parses to at least one mapping, and is not
*wholly* degenerate. That last clause matters: a `/ToUnicode` mapping every code
to U+0000 or the Private Use Area is worse than none, because strategy 1 in §7.2
succeeds, the chain stops, and text extracts as nulls. 53 fonts fail exactly that
way, and counting them as coverage would have flattered the number by four
points.

## Two populations, not one

| Corpus | fonts | usable |
|---|---:|---:|
| Generated by today's LaTeX (MiKTeX 25.12) | 47 | **100.0%** |
| The wild (pdf.js test corpus) | 1343 | **51.4%** |

Every one of the thirteen LaTeX samples — Computer Modern, T1/cm-super, Latin
Modern, URW Times, microtype, maths, and OpenType on both Unicode engines —
produced complete `/ToUnicode`. Including the plain `\documentclass{article}`
default that the spec singles out as the problem case.

The reason is visible in the version numbers:

| pdfTeX | fonts | usable |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1.40.10 – 1.40.21 | 22 | **0.0%** |
| 1.40.25 – 1.40.26 | 6 | **83%** (5 of 6) |

pdfTeX started emitting `/ToUnicode` automatically. The defect the spec was
written against **has been fixed upstream**. What remains is an archive: PDFs are
a thirty-year format, and a library that edits existing documents meets the
documents that already exist. The operative number is 53%, not 100%.

## Where the failures actually are

By font kind:

| kind | fonts | usable |
|---|---:|---:|
| Type1 | 500 | **17.2%** |
| TrueType | 432 | 54.2% |
| Type0 | 458 | **91.0%** |

Composite fonts are essentially solved. Type1 is the problem, which is
consistent with the age story: Type1 is the old format.

## The part that changes the plan

The spec assumes the fallback for a missing `/ToUnicode` is glyph-name
heuristics, because "subset fonts produced by LaTeX are the standard failure
case: no `/ToUnicode`, and glyph names like `g34`."

For the 653 fonts without usable `/ToUnicode`, here is what §7.2's later
strategies actually have to work with:

| available | fonts | share of failures |
|---|---:|---:|
| `/Differences` with glyph names (step 2) | 308 | 47.2% |
| base encoding only (steps 2–3) | 225 | 34.5% |
| predefined CMap (step 4) | 42 | 6.4% |
| nothing at the PDF level (steps 5–6) | 78 | 11.9% |

And of those 653, what the glyph names look like:

| glyph names | fonts |
|---|---:|
| Adobe Glyph List names | 300 |
| no `/Differences` at all | 346 |
| `uniXXXX` | 1 |
| **opaque (`g34`, `cidNN`)** | **3** |
| mixed | 3 |

**Six fonts in 1390 — 0.4% — carry the opaque glyph names the spec expected to
be the standard failure case.** They are not LaTeX Type1 subsets at all; they
are TrueType subsets from Office-class producers (`EMMOLK+Cambria`,
`GRDDWT+Arial-BoldMT`, `TtkvjyMinionPro-Regular`).

Meanwhile the 78 fonts with "nothing at the PDF level" have **no `/Differences`
whatsoever** — so glyph-name heuristics have nothing to chew on either. Their
route is step 5: reverse lookup through the embedded font's own `cmap` table.

## What this decides

**Steps 1–4 are Phase 3, and they are the whole game.** `/ToUnicode` parsing,
`/Encoding` + `/Differences` + the Adobe Glyph List, base encodings, and
predefined CMaps together reach about 99.6% of embedded fonts.

**The Adobe Glyph List is the load-bearing component**, and the spec mentions it
only in passing. 300 of the 653 failures resolve through it and nothing else.
It must be complete — the full AGL plus the `uniXXXX`/`uXXXXX` conventions —
rather than a partial table of common names. This is the single highest-value
item in §7.2 and it was not identified as such.

**Step 5 (reverse `cmap` lookup) is Phase 3**, not Phase 8. It is the only route
for the 78 fonts with no PDF-level encoding, and it is unavoidable because
nothing else addresses them.

**Step 6 (glyph-name heuristics) can be Phase 3 but is cheap and low-stakes.**
It is worth about 0.4% of embedded fonts. The `uniXXXX` and `gNN` patterns are a
few dozen lines, so build them with step 5 — but the spec's fear that this is
where a quarter goes is not supported.

**A shape-matching fallback is not justified.** The spec floats it as "possibly";
the data says the residue after steps 1–5 is six fonts. That work belongs
nowhere on the roadmap until a corpus shows otherwise.

## Confidence and bias

The wild-corpus half is mozilla/pdf.js's test suite, which is a corpus of *hard
cases* — files kept precisely because they broke a renderer. It over-represents
broken and unusual files, so 51.4% understates real-world coverage. A
representative sample would land higher, though the age argument means it would
still be well under 85%.

That bias runs in a helpful direction for the decision that matters. A hard-case
corpus should *overstate* the need for exotic fallbacks, and it still shows
opaque glyph names on 0.4% of fonts. If shape matching were ever going to look
necessary, this is the corpus where it would.

Two gaps remain, both recorded in `corpus/manifest.toml`:

- No Word, InDesign, or LibreOffice files beyond what pdf.js happens to contain.
  The Office-class producers are where the opaque-name outliers came from, so a
  proper sample could move that 0.4%.
- The "(no producer)" bucket is 274 fonts at 54.4% coverage and is a mix of
  everything, including files stripped by `pdftk`.

Neither changes the architectural conclusion; both would sharpen the percentages.

## Method

`harness/fontsurvey` walks every `/Type /Font` object, skipping the descendants
of Type0 fonts so composite fonts are counted once. A font counts as embedded
when its descriptor carries `/FontFile`, `/FontFile2`, or `/FontFile3`; Type 3
fonts are excluded, having no font program. Its `/ToUnicode` is decoded and
parsed with a minimal `bfchar`/`bfrange` reader that resolves surrogate pairs
and array destinations.

That reader lives in the harness, not the library, deliberately. The production
parser belongs in `rasura-layout` and has more to do; keeping the
measurement tool separate stops it from quietly becoming the implementation.

The AGL column is an upper bound: it counts names that are *not* `uniXXXX` and
*not* `gNN`-shaped, without checking them against the actual Adobe Glyph List,
which is Phase 3 work. If some of those 300 turn out to be unrecognised names,
the conclusion strengthens rather than weakens — more work lands on steps 5 and
6, which is exactly the risk to re-measure once the AGL exists.
